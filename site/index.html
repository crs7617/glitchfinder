Method & limits

For tied-embedding models (where input and output embeddings share weights), we compute the cosine distance of each token's embedding from a reference centroid of known-unused tokens. Tokens close to this "garbage direction" are ranked as suspicious.

For untied models, weight decay during training pushes unused input embeddings toward zero. We rank by L2 norm: lower norm means more suspicious.

Verification loads the full model (only feasible for models ≤ 1 B parameters on CPU with 8 GB RAM) and tests each candidate with three prompt templates designed to elicit the token. If the model assigns the token less than 1% probability across all prompts, it is confirmed as a glitch.

What was verified

Of the five models analyzed, only Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B was verified: 19 non-flagged candidates were tested and all 19 confirmed as glitches (max probability < 0.003%). The remaining four models — Qwen2.5-0.5B, SmolLM2-360M, GPT-2, and Phi-1.5 — are indicator-only. Their top-ranked tokens are candidates, not confirmed glitches.

Limits

  • Requires safetensors weights. Models with only .bin files are skipped.
  • Runs on CPU with 8 GB RAM. Very large embedding matrices may not fit.
  • Verification is limited to models ≤ 1 B parameters (float32).
  • Indicators are heuristics. A low-norm or centroid-proximate token may be legitimate but rare.
  • Three prompt templates cannot cover every context where a token might appear.

Methodology from Land & Bartolo (2024), "Fishing for Magikarp" (arXiv 2405.05417). Independent implementation.
